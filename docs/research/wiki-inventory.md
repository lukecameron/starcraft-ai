# StarCraft AI wiki inventory (bounded crawl)

**Crawl date:** 2026-09-20 (Australia/Sydney)
**Scope:** StarCraft AI wiki index plus a small set of primary pages and linked primary sources covering terrain/topology, combat simulation, build-order search, opponent modeling, replay tooling, and selective ML. This is an idea index, not a literature review. The wiki is old in places; current compatibility and license claims need repository-level checks.

## Cross-track conclusions

1. **Terrain/BWEM:** highest-confidence reusable idea. Cache a map graph of areas, bases, chokes and paths; keep the feature surface narrow and versioned. BWEM’s BWAPI 4.1.2 requirement is the principal portability risk. BWTA-style polygons/build placement can remain a later fallback.
2. **Combat/SparCraft:** useful for cheap tactical candidate ranking, with explicit sim-to-game calibration. Missing collisions, fog, spells and randomness make it unsafe as an authoritative evaluator, especially for large engagements.
3. **Build-order search:** the crawl found evidence for replay labels and historical build-order execution, but no current, verified BW-specific search implementation worth adopting immediately. Start with a small hand-authored portfolio and add search only when a fast forward model and a measurable opening failure justify it.
4. **Opponent modeling:** begin with a transparent rush/expand/tech hypothesis tracker using legal observations. A learned or graphical model earns inclusion only after replay extraction and held-out calibration show value over priors.
5. **Replay tooling:** the wiki’s catalog is valuable for schema design but too stale to trust as a dependency list. Preserve raw replays and incremental metadata from the first match; validate parser compatibility on 1.16.1/OpenBW outputs before bulk collection.
6. **Selective ML:** the best early candidates are opening selection and opponent hypothesis calibration. Keep inference to a small CPU-friendly model and compare against a rule-based baseline with a held-out split. Avoid deep models, large datasets and privileged features until the match loop and data provenance are stable.

## Deferred frontier

Revisit BWTA2 polygons and advanced wall/building placement; NUSBot-style influence maps and transport/ambush planning; Bayesian/Markov-field opponent models; replay corpus acquisition and deduplication; SparCraft search policies beyond tactical probes; benchmark launcher porting; SARSA/reinforcement learning; and the wiki’s old language wrappers/tools. These are deferred because current evidence does not establish compatibility, licensing, or measurable strength per unit of work.

## First three experiments

1. **Terrain probe:** import/cache BWEM on two representative maps; report compile status, analysis time, graph size, path/choke query cost, and OpenBW/API compatibility.
2. **Combat probe:** replay 20 fixed small engagements through SparCraft and the actual engine; report winner agreement, damage/kill error, and disagreement cases caused by collisions or omitted mechanics.
3. **Information probe:** implement rush/expand/tech hypotheses from visibility-limited events and score predictions against replay labels at fixed times; require improvement over a majority prior before connecting the model to strategy selection.

## Crawl limits and uncertainty

This was a bounded crawl of the wiki index and approximately ten primary pages/linked sources. No replay, dataset, binary, or image assets were downloaded. Several wiki links are old, timing/API claims are historical, and repository licenses/buildability were not exhaustively verified. The next pass should verify exact revisions and licenses only for components selected by the experiments above.
